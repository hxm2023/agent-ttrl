\section{Introduction}
Deployed tool-using agents receive feedback that is partial, endogenous,
and often unreliable: an API receipt may report success while the
underlying state did not change; a verifier may agree with a wrong
consensus; and the agent's own choices determine what evidence it sees.
A natural goal is \emph{deployment-period improvement}: let the agent
update its policy from the tasks it encounters, so that later, unseen
tasks in the same domain are handled better. This is test-time
reinforcement learning (TTRL)~\cite{ttrl2025} moved from unlabeled math
reasoning to stateful tool use.

The literature has moved fast. Test-time self-consistency rewards
improve math reasoning~\cite{ttrl2025} and can be stabilized with tool
verification~\cite{t3rl2026}, but they amplify wrong consensus and even
harmful behavior when unverified~\cite{amplification2026}. Context
engineering~\cite{ace2025}, memory RL~\cite{memopilot2026}, action-layer
bandits~\cite{olivia2026}, and syntactic adaptation
vectors~\cite{gtta2026} adapt deployed agents without touching weights.
Counterfactual machinery exists for offline trace
repair~\cite{causalflow2026} and general RL credit~\cite{shapley2026},
and commit gates are studied for self-evolving
systems~\cite{pace2026,vag2026}. What is missing is a closed loop that
combines deployment-period parameter updates, partial-evidence credit,
risk-controlled commit, and prequential evaluation of future transfer
under one budget and identity discipline.

This paper builds and evaluates that loop. We contribute:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Protocol machinery}: evidence tiers (hard/soft in
  adaptation; hidden evaluator never), prequential induction as the
  primary metric, three-channel budget accounting, and policy identity
  binding on a guarded rollout--update chain.
\item \textbf{EGC-TTRL}: evidence-gated counterfactual test-time RL --
  paired counterfactual branches at selected decision states produce
  signed, reliability-gated action credit that drives action-token LoRA
  updates; a risk-controlled commit gate (empirical-Bernstein e-process)
  decides whether a candidate adapter reaches future tasks.
\item \textbf{An honest empirical synthesis}: across CTS, AppWorld, and
  tau2 with two model families, the protocol machinery runs
  end-to-end, SafeCommit eliminates catastrophic updates in stress
  simulation, and deployment-period LoRA-RL does not produce a stable
  inductive-transfer gain at the scales tested -- a reproducible negative
  result with an open harness.
\end{itemize}
