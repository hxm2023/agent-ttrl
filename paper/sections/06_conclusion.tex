\section{Conclusion}
We built and evaluated a closed loop for deployment-period improvement
of stateful tool agents: evidence tiers that keep the hidden evaluator
out of learning, prequential induction as the primary metric, a
three-channel budget ledger, guarded rollout identities, paired-branch
evidence credit with reliability gating, and a risk-controlled commit
gate. The machinery runs end-to-end on three environments with two
model families, and SafeCommit eliminates catastrophic updates in
stress simulation while remaining non-degenerate.

Our empirical synthesis is honest: across CTS, AppWorld, and tau2,
deployment-period LoRA-RL updates did not produce a stable
inductive-future-transfer gain at the scales and update magnitudes we
ran. Directions flipped with task pools. Weak single-task updates had
negligible behavioral footprint; stronger updates (16 rollouts, 8
steps, lr $5\times10^{-5}$, tau2 16-task matched control) change
behavior measurably on subsequent tasks in both directions without a
net stable gain. We therefore stop the algorithm headline per our
pre-registered stop rule, and contribute the protocol machinery, the
commit-gate result, and a reproducible harness with all manifests. The
immediate next step is stronger backbones and longer streams, where the
partial-match signal on tau2 (18--25\% on Mistral-7B) indicates a
trainable space exists.
